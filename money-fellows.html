Made savings goals easier to understand and act on.

01 / Executive summary

I connected several product surfaces into one clearer decision journey.

Money Fellows digitizes the Egyptian gam'eya, a savings circle in which members contribute monthly and receive the full pot at different points in the cycle. Slot timing changes both customer value and risk. That means the interface must explain more than a product. It must explain commitment, eligibility, limits, timing, and the next action without creating false confidence.

The challenge

Intent was spread across disconnected terms and states.

A customer could want a goal without understanding how progress, a recommended circle, eligibility, and joining connected.

My role

Design the rules and the experience together.

I mapped states and edge cases, shaped journeys and language, prototyped flows, aligned teams, and stayed through implementation review.

The approach

Give every state one clear meaning and one useful next step.

The work used consistent vocabulary, contextual recommendations, progressive explanation, and explicit risk communication.

The result

Customers moved further from goal creation into product action.

Goals reached 12% organic engagement, with 61.1% converting to goal creation and 69.8% progressing into the join flow.

02 / Product model

The slot is the product decision.

Everyone in a circle contributes the same total. What changes is when each member receives the pot. Earlier slots behave more like access to credit. Later slots behave more like structured saving and may include rewards. The customer-facing journey therefore has to connect aspiration, affordability, eligibility, and timing before asking for commitment.

01 / IntentName the goal and amount.
02 / PlanSee progress and the remaining gap.
03 / FitUnderstand the relevant circle and eligibility.
04 / ActionJoin with clear terms and next steps.

This is an original abstraction, not a product screen. Public product facts are attributed to Money Fellows. Interface screens remain withheld pending asset-level clearance.

03 / Key work

Four workstreams, one design principle: make the next decision legible.

Goals V2

Defined one reusable financial vocabulary.

I brought Goal, Received, Scheduled, and Short or Extra into a consistent coverage model across Home, My Goals, and Goal Details. Customers no longer had to relearn the meaning of progress on each screen.

Recommendations

Connected a recommendation to the customer's stated goal.

The product prompt became a contextual next move rather than a generic offer. The design reduced the distance between seeing a relevant circle and acting on it.

Onboarding

Taught the model at the moment it became useful.

Instead of front-loading explanation, each step carried one decision and the minimum context needed to make it. The flow stayed clear for new customers without slowing down experienced ones.

Migration

Designed the transition, not only the destination.

Under a timeline of less than two weeks, I helped align state-specific messaging, dependencies, edge cases, and handoffs so customers understood what changed and what to do next.

04 / Risk and delivery

Trust came from accuracy, not reassurance.

Eligibility, limits, KYC, and credit communication are not secondary copy problems. They are product states with legal and emotional weight. I worked with product, engineering, and risk to define what each state meant, what it could promise, and what the customer could do next.

  1. 01
    Map the rule before the screen.

    Agree the valid states, dependencies, and failure cases before visual design begins.

  2. 02
    Make the decision explicit.

    Say what happened, why the customer is seeing it, and whether anything can change.

  3. 03
    Specify the difficult cases.

    Write the states that are easy to miss in a happy-path prototype and costly to discover in production.

  4. 04
    Stay through implementation.

    Review the built experience and correct the gaps where logic, language, and interface drift apart.

05 / Research

Research was used to change the route, not decorate the case study.

Insights from 70+ customer interviews and behavioral analysis shaped the onboarding sequence, terminology, and points of explanation. The work continued through experiments and iteration rather than treating launch as the end of discovery.

Listen, frame a hypothesis, prototype the decision, and let the next round of evidence correct the last.
