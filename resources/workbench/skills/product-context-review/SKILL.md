# Product context review

Make the next product decision fit the people, tasks, and operating conditions it must serve. Start from the current brief, research, raw feedback, and accepted decisions. Keep this review proportional to the uncertainty; a clear, narrow task can proceed directly.

## Review the decision

1. Name the decision and the job. What is being chosen, for whom, and what observable user outcome would improve? Separate an evidenced need from a competitor feature, stakeholder request, or assumption.
2. Identify the context that could change the answer. Use relevant evidence about task, role, market, language, existing workflow, payment or trust expectations, and applicable requirements. Record the source and gaps. Nationality and a colleague's experience can suggest research questions; neither establishes what all users need.
3. Check whether the reference transfers. Identify the useful principle and the assumptions behind the reference's feature or flow. Compare those assumptions with this product. Preserve useful ambition or craft while changing the parts that do not fit; similarity alone proves neither suitability nor failure.
4. Read beneath feedback labels and averages. Inspect actual comments before calling an issue translation, usability, or missing functionality. Where data permits, compare relevant cohorts using compatible time windows, metric definitions, denominators, and sample sizes. Investigate different rating interpretations through evidence. Report uncertainty and avoid inferring causes from a pooled score or a release coinciding with churn.
5. Choose the next useful action. Recommend a change, an improvement to an existing flow, a prototype, or a focused evidence check according to the decision's stakes and uncertainty. State what observation would change the recommendation. Do not require slower work or a research project when the available evidence supports action.

## Learn or deliver

State whether the next artifact tests an uncertain direction or delivers a committed improvement. For a learning test, define the question and the observation needed to answer it. Label prototype status and material limits such as mocked data or missing integrations; polished appearance and working interactions do not establish production readiness.

For delivery, choose the smallest coherent change that can be reviewed and checked. Preserve the project's engineering, reliability, security, and release requirements. Consider reversibility when comparing approaches, and avoid burying independent fixes in a large change. A demonstrated, authorized small fix can proceed without inventing a discovery phase.

Use the project's approved context and components where available, including in prototypes. Keep ownership of the result: verify the intended outcome, record what happened and any follow-up, and communicate status through the authorized project channel. Fix a recurring process problem where it originates when the evidence supports that change. Neither faster work nor this skill grants new access, permission to contact customers, or permission to release.

For an AI conversation or decision flow, also test whether people can tell what the system did, understand the basis of a material decision, take a clear next action, and recover from failure. Choose disclosure, explanation, consent, logs, and human routes from the actual task, risk, and verified requirements; a talk's fintech example is not a legal checklist for every product.

## Make it usable

Match the requested deliverable. Advice can remain in the response; create or edit a project artifact when the task calls for one. Include only the decision-record details that help choose and verify the next action:

- Decision, intended user outcome, and recommendation.
- Evidence and important context differences, with sources; label assumptions and gaps.
- What stays, what changes, and the reason.
- Next action, its learning or delivery status, and the result that would support or overturn the choice.

When preparing a handoff or implementation, put confirmed requirements into the current brief or product spec with their provenance. Use `agent-task-brief` if the handoff needs clearer scope; use the project's design system for implementation. This review does not create a second source of truth or replace visual reference research, component rules, or release authorization.
