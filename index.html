02 / About

I studied engineering first, which is why I read the rule before the screen.

Four years designing consumer and B2B products across fintech, HR software, social commerce and AI-enabled ventures. The thread is complexity that cannot be simplified away: a limit set by a risk model, a policy that differs for every client, a prediction that is only sometimes right. Someone still has to face that on a screen and decide what to do next.

I am most useful where customer behaviour, business constraints and system logic meet, and I stay with the work through specification, implementation review and QA. A design that is correct in Figma and wrong in production is not finished.

04 / AI in the practice

AI is a power tool in the work, not the headline.

I use AI for the parts of design that are mostly synthesis and repetition, so more of my attention goes to the judgment that still has to be human: what to build, for whom, and why. The point is a better first draft and a clearer decision, not a longer list of tools.

  1. Research synthesis

    Turn raw research into a point of view.

    Clustering interview notes, tagging recurring patterns, and pressure-testing themes, so a stack of sessions becomes a structured argument I can design against instead of a folder I never reopen.

  2. Planning

    Make a fuzzy brief scoped and testable.

    Mapping flows, drafting acceptance criteria, and walking the edge cases before a screen exists, so the team argues about the right problem early rather than discovering it in build.

  3. Prototyping

    Get a direction into something you can feel.

    Standing up rough, clickable flows and copy variants quickly, so a concept can be critiqued in the hand rather than defended in the abstract, and the weak version gets found sooner.

  4. Product operations

    Keep the decision legible after handoff.

    Drafting specifications, release notes and decision records, and keeping cross-functional context tidy, so the reasoning behind a design survives the distance to production.

Independent experiment

Playful

Outside client work I build small, self-directed projects to keep my hands in the material and understand where AI-native interaction genuinely helps and where it just adds noise. Playful is one of them: exploratory by design, held to real judgment rather than novelty, and shared here as an ongoing experiment rather than a finished result.
