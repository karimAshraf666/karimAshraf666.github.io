02 / Positioning

Product design is my primary lane. Product ownership is the adjacent value.

I am not presenting one vague hybrid title. The distinction is deliberate: design is the craft I lead with; product ownership is where my systems thinking, scope judgment, and delivery work create additional leverage.

Primary fit

Senior Product Designer

I research, structure, prototype, and refine the experience itself. I care about the interface, the behavior underneath it, and whether the built product matches the decision we made.

  • Customer research and behavioral analysis
  • Interaction design and information architecture
  • Complex states, systems, and trust-sensitive UX
  • Prototyping, handoff, implementation review, and QA

Evidence: eligibility, limits, and credit communication at Money Fellows and two device contexts at Bluworks.

Adjacent fit

Product Owner / Product Manager

I fit ownership roles when the work needs someone who can translate customer evidence and business rules into a focused scope, align teams, and stay accountable through delivery.

  • Problem framing and opportunity definition
  • Business-rule mapping and edge-case decisions
  • Prioritization, specification, and stakeholder alignment
  • Experiment design, metrics, and cross-functional delivery

Evidence: scope, boundaries, and the operator console on Playful, plus a migration delivered in under two weeks across product, engineering, risk, and CRM.

04 / About

Engineering trained me to read the rule before drawing the screen.

I came to product design through engineering, which is probably why I enjoy the knotty parts of a product: the rule behind the screen, the edge case that changes the journey, or the AI response that needs a thoughtful fallback. For more than four years, I have worked across consumer and B2B products in regulated services, SaaS, social commerce, growth, and AI-enabled ventures.

I like being close to the whole journey, from the first uncertain question to the details of what ships. A polished Figma file matters, but only if it helps a customer move with confidence and gives the team something honest and buildable.

I keep sharpening the craft through UXcel, using its assessments, courses, and project feedback to challenge how I think about research, interaction, content, product strategy, visual design, and leadership.

When I am away from screens, I am usually making something with my hands, puzzling my way up a bouldering wall, or finding an excuse to be near the sea. Animals win me over immediately, although my two cats already have first claim on my attention and, apparently, most of the house.
