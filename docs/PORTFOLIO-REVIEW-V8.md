# Portfolio Review V8 | Recruiter and Product Leadership Pass

Date: 2026-08-26

This review was completed against the live GitHub Pages site, the local repository, Karim's 2026 résumé, the authenticated LinkedIn profile, the public UXcel project grid, and the prior Claude task named `Karim's portfolio overhaul`.

## Core diagnosis

The previous portfolio had a strong editorial visual language and unusually careful evidence hygiene. Its main weakness was communication speed. A recruiter could see craft, but had to work too hard to answer four basic questions:

1. What product problem was Karim solving?
2. What did Karim personally own?
3. What decision changed because of his work?
4. What evidence shows that the work mattered?

The copy often used thoughtful but abstract headlines before stating the concrete product action. Money Fellows contained 2,125 words without a product image. Bluworks contained 2,535 words and 42 images. Both pages rewarded a patient reader, but portfolio visitors are usually scanning first and reading second.

## What success looks like

Every case study should be understandable at three depths:

- 15 seconds: title, role, problem, and outcome.
- 90 seconds: executive summary, key decisions, and evidence.
- 5 minutes: enough detail to judge systems thinking, design craft, and delivery ownership.

A strong section follows this order:

1. State the customer or business problem in plain language.
2. State Karim's responsibility using a verb.
3. Show the decision or interface change.
4. Attach evidence or state clearly when evidence is qualitative.
5. Explain what the work demonstrates about Karim.

## Copy standard

### Prefer

- "Made savings goals easier to understand and act on."
- "Connected one financial vocabulary across Home, My Goals, and Goal Details."
- "Designed a shared tablet for a queue of workers, not a personal session."
- "Put a live group on the home screen so customers could see the social mechanic before joining."
- "Exposed AI confidence and added a human review step before the result became a business record."

### Avoid

- Rhetorical headlines that delay the subject.
- Metaphors that require a second paragraph to decode.
- Process language without a product decision.
- Long galleries without explaining why each screen is included.
- Repeating the same point in the headline, body, caption, and outcome section.
- Using a number as proof when its source or attribution is unclear.

## Homepage journey

The line "The rules are complicated. The choice should not" was visually memorable but incomplete as positioning. It described a philosophy, not Karim's role, level, industry value, or product range. Project cards also used mostly abstract visuals, which made the work feel less tangible than the public UXcel material.

2. Current context: Product Designer at Money Fellows, Cairo, available selectively.
3. Four projects with real covers or an intentionally abstract confidential cover.
4. Evidence on every card, including clear disclosure when evidence is qualitative.
5. Explicit distinction between senior product design and adjacent product ownership fit.
6. Compact experience record, About, résumé, and direct contact actions.

## Product Designer versus Product Owner or Product Manager

Karim should not present as a vague hybrid. The market should understand the hierarchy:

- Primary lane: Senior Product Designer. This is where research, interaction design, information architecture, visual judgment, product systems, prototyping, implementation review, and QA create the strongest differentiation.
- Adjacent lane: Product Owner or Product Manager roles that need customer evidence translated into scope, business rules, prioritization, stakeholder decisions, specifications, delivery, and metrics.

The differentiator is not "designer who also likes strategy." It is the ability to carry a complex product problem from evidence and system logic into a shippable experience, while keeping customer, business, and engineering constraints aligned.

## Case-study decisions

### Money Fellows

- Lead with the customer decision, not the philosophy of goals.
- Explain the money-circle and slot model once, in direct language.
- Group Goals, recommendations, onboarding, and migration under one throughline.
- Move reported outcomes into a dedicated evidence band.
- Keep product screens unpublished until cleared asset by asset.
- State that the original diagrams are abstractions and not product interfaces.

### Bluworks

- Make the two contexts explicit in the first screen.
- Curate the 42-image page to the strongest web and tablet evidence.
- Explain configuration and daily operation as a pair.
- Explain why a shared tablet changes identity, speed, privacy, and recovery.
- Link to the full UXcel libraries for readers who want exhaustive material.

### Sharwa

- Lead with the unfamiliar behavior: commit, depend on others, and act before expiry.
- Show how live group cards, a numbered progression, and direct price comparison solved specific points of confusion.
- Keep the attribution caveat beside the reported product movement.

### 1MORETHING Ventures

- Explain that these are scoped venture directions, not scaled products.
- Make AI failure handling the shared product principle.
- Give each venture one problem, one core design decision, and selected evidence.
- Preserve the NOMU Group attribution on JumlatyPro.
- Avoid production, adoption, or revenue language that cannot be evidenced.

## Visual standard

- Reduce the headline scale so the first viewport can contain meaning, not only typography.
- Keep one dominant action per viewport.
- Use project covers from public UXcel material where available.
- Use an original abstract Money Fellows cover because the interface is confidential.
- Curate screens by decision, not by completeness.
- Keep reading width between 650 and 900 pixels.
- Use consistent section labels, fact rows, decision cards, captions, and outcome bands.
- Maintain keyboard navigation, visible focus, reduced-motion support, and responsive layouts.

## Acceptance criteria

- A recruiter can identify Karim's role, level, and domains from the first viewport.
- Every project card states a problem, contribution, and evidence signal.
- Every case study has an executive summary before detailed narrative.
- Product design is clearly primary; product ownership is clearly adjacent.
- No Money Fellows interface image is published.
- No metric exceeds the résumé, LinkedIn, or evidence inventory.
- No em dash appears in HTML or CSS.
- All internal links, external showcase links, images, and résumé downloads resolve.
- Pages have no horizontal overflow at desktop and mobile widths.
- The full experience remains readable with JavaScript disabled.
